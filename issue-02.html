The 12-Week Study Plan

Aim for about 5–7 hours a week. Falling behind is normal — just shift the schedule, don't quit. Each week ends with something you've built or understood, not just watched.

WeekFocusWhat you'll be able to do
1–2Python fundamentalsWrite small scripts; use lists, loops, functions, and notebooks comfortably.
3Data with Pandas & NumPyLoad a CSV, explore it, and make a simple chart.
4–5Core ML with scikit-learnTrain your first model to predict something; understand train/test splits.
6Math intuitionExplain vectors, gradients, and probability in plain English.
7–8Neural networksBuild a small network that classifies images (e.g. handwritten digits).
9How LLMs workDescribe tokens, embeddings, and transformers at a high level.
10Prompting & AI APIsCall the Claude or OpenAI API from Python and get useful output.
11Build: chat over your docs (RAG)Make an AI answer questions about a PDF you give it.
12Capstone projectShip one small thing end-to-end and share it.

Curated Resources (Start with the Free Ones)

Free · Course

Andrew Ng — "AI For Everyone" (Coursera)

Non-technical, big-picture intro. Perfect first watch to understand what AI can and can't do.

Free · Course

Kaggle "Learn" Micro-Courses

Short, hands-on lessons on Python, Pandas, and Intro to ML — all in your browser, no setup.

Free · Course

fast.ai — "Practical Deep Learning for Coders"

Build real models from day one. Best when you're ready for Phase 3.

Free · Video

3Blue1Brown — "Neural Networks" series (YouTube)

The clearest visual explanation of how neural nets actually work. Watch when the math feels abstract.

Paid · Book

"Hands-On Machine Learning" by Aurélien Géron

The go-to practical book. Worth buying once you're past the basics and want depth.

Free · Docs

Anthropic & OpenAI API Quickstarts

When you reach Phase 5, the official docs are the fastest way to make your first AI-powered app.

Glossary — Keep This Handy

Model
A program that has learned patterns from data and can make predictions or generate output.
Training
The process of showing a model lots of examples so it adjusts itself to get better.
Dataset
The collection of examples a model learns from (e.g. thousands of labeled photos).
Token
A chunk of text (roughly a word-piece) that language models read and write in.
Prompt
The instruction or question you give an AI model to get a response.
Embedding
Turning text or images into lists of numbers so a computer can compare meaning.
RAG
Retrieval-Augmented Generation — letting an AI look things up in your documents before answering.
Fine-tuning
Taking an existing model and training it a bit more on your specific data.
Agent
An AI system that can take actions (use tools, call APIs) to accomplish a goal, not just chat.

5 Habits That Make It Stick

  • Code along, don't just watch. Type every example yourself — passive watching fades fast.
  • Build in public. Share progress on GitHub or socials; explaining cements learning.
  • Embrace being confused. Confusion is the feeling of learning. Sit with it before searching.
  • Use AI as a tutor. Ask Claude to explain errors, quiz you, or break down hard ideas.
  • Ship small & often. A finished tiny project beats a perfect unfinished one.

Your move

Open a notebook today and write three lines of Python. That's it. The roadmap in Issue No. 01 tells you where you're going; this issue tells you how to walk it. Start small, stay consistent, and in twelve weeks you'll be building things you can't imagine today.
